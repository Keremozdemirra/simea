# Determinism and the Predictability of Financial Futures

**Status:** positioning note. No draft yet.

## The question

If determinism is true — if every decision is in principle knowable — does it
follow that the financial future is predictable?

The paper argues that it does not, and that the failure is structural rather
than a shortfall of data or compute. It then argues that predictability, in
markets, is better understood as a variable that moves inversely with autonomy.

## Where it stands

The general limits on prediction (chaos, computational irreducibility,
self-prediction) and the finance-specific ones (self-consuming predictability,
Grossman–Stiglitz, reflexivity, the Lucas critique) are established results.
Assembled well they are a literature review, not a contribution.

The contribution, if there is one, is the autonomy–predictability trade-off in
§4 and the erosion dynamic in §4.1. Both are stated as propositions so they can
be attacked.

Two decisions are open and are recorded in §8: whether the determinism premise
is load-bearing or merely a frame, and whether this is a theory paper now or an
empirical paper after the measurement problem is solved.
